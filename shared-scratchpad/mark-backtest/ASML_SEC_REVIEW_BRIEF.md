## Downside signals

1. **Receivables are outrunning sales**: AR +140.0% over six months and +45.1% YoY (4,996.9 → 7,252.6) vs Q2 sales +21.3% YoY; implied DSO **59.1 → 70.8 days YoY (+11.7 days)** (AR ÷ (quarterly sales/91)). The WC line absorbed 6,772.7 of H1 cash — larger than the entire quarter's revenue growth in absolute cash terms.
2. **H1 cash conversion is negative**: OCF −482.5 vs +689.1 a year ago (swing −1,171.6) while Q1'26 alone was −2,185.6; cash nearly halved (12,916.0 → 6,671.9). Q4 collection lumpiness (Q4'25 OCF +11,410.3) flatters trailing-twelve numbers — the H1-vs-H1 read is the honest one.
3. **The guided step-up must execute into cash**: €11.0–12.0B needs +18–29% sequential delivery **and** 55–57% GM (a level no recent quarter reached); any push-out of a logic/memory customer order hits both the print and the receivables conversion simultaneously.
4. **The filing's own risk enumeration** (EX-99.3 forward-looking section, observed 2026-09-23): "risks relating to the realization of our backlog, the risk of order cancellations, delays or push outs and restrictions on shipments of systems under export controls… the impact of the tariff announcements", plus semiconductor inventory levels and customer capacity pace — all named by the registrant, not inferred.
5. **Capital return sized for the old cash**: 3,733.0 returned in H1 against −482.5 of H1 OCF — sustainable from the cash balance, but it is what shrank net cash by ~€5.0B; a weak Q3 would force the question of return pacing (EX-99.1 dividend/buyback section: ~€1.1B bought back in Q2, €1.88 interim dividend paid 2026-08-05).
